The posture checks

Ten checks, mapped to real attacks

wifi_encryptionhigh
Open, WEP, WPA1 or TKIP links.
captive_portalmedium
Portals that intercept and redirect traffic.
tls_interceptioncritical
MITM via untrusted or private-CA certificates.
proxy_injectionmedium
Transparent proxies rewriting plain HTTP.
dns_integrityhigh
Hijacked answers vs. a trusted DoH resolver.
router_exposuremedium
Telnet, UPnP and admin ports on the gateway.
arp_gatewayhigh
Spoofed ARP entries and rogue gateways.
mac_randomizationlow
Is your hardware address randomised?
evil_twinhigh
Same SSID advertised with different security.
reachabilityinfo
Context: is there egress at all?
Checks degrade to SKIP when a probe cannot run, so a blocked network never produces a false alarm.

Verdict & scoring

From ten results to one decision

0 · unsafe45 · caution80 · safe100
WEIGHTED

Severity penalties

Failures subtract from 100 by severity: critical 45, high 25, medium 14, low 7. Warnings cost a fraction.

CAPPED

Can't average out

Any critical failure caps the score at 25. Any high failure caps it at 55. Severe findings stay severe.

EXPLAINED

Reasons and action

A plain verdict, the ordered problems, and one recommended action per finding.

example: open SSID + TLS interception + DNS hijack + evil twin → score 0/100 → UNSAFE

Honesty & roadmap

What it does well, and what comes next

Known limitations
  • Evil-twin detection is heuristic; a perfect clone needs beacon fingerprinting.
  • TLS checks trust the system CA store, so an admin-installed proxy CA is a warning.
  • Checks reflect the moment of connection.
Roadmap
  • Passive 802.11 beacon and RTT fingerprinting for stronger evil-twin detection.
  • DNS-over-TLS enforcement and per-process connection attribution.
  • Tray indicator and a fleet agent for small organizations.
